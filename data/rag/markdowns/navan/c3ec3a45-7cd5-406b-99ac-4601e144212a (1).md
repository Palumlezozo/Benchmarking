# Introduction

# The Navan Business Travel Index (BTI)

A new industry benchmark using Navan's unique, proprietary volume and spend-based data, this quarterly report provides profound insights into business travel. The Navan BTI is intended to be a national and global indicator of the strength of the business travel economy. Unlike some traditional travel indices, it isolates business travel activity from consumer travel patterns.

The Navan BTI is grounded in proprietary, volume, and spend-based data derived from Navan's leading all-in-one business travel and expense management platform. This report is designed to enable investors, finance leaders, and decision-makers to gain objective, data-driven insights into business travel investments. In the face of macroeconomic uncertainty, companies continue to prioritize face-to-face connections, driving 15% year-over-year growth in business travel activity.* This growth reveals what we've long believed at Navan — virtual meetings have their place, but there's no replacement for face-to-face connections.

Validated by the Nasdaq® Economic Research Team, the Navan BTI offers a look into corporate travel trends, information that can be difficult to access through traditional market indicators. Now, organizations can benchmark their travel strategies against a verified index rooted in business travel activity, gaining visibility into which industries are focusing most on in-person connections, which parts of the world are seeing concentrated business travel activity, and how corporate travel rhythms shift throughout the year.

# The Seasonal Patterns of Business Travel

The Navan Business Travel Index data exposes a clear inverse relationship between seasonal business and leisure travel — an insight that we think is often overlooked. During peak leisure travel periods, such as summer and major holidays, business travel volume on our platform consistently declines. Conversely, business travel activity on our platform rises during the spring and fall, periods which correspond with corporate planning cycles and industry conferences.

This seasonality reflects established patterns in both business and leisure travel. Our data shows that corporate travel activity typically escalates in the spring, which blurs Q1, as we see companies kick-start annual initiatives and build critical relationships. Activity then peaks again in September, which we believe is driven by conference season and planning for the coming year. It then tapers off around the holidays in November and December, when leisure travel reaches its highest point.

By separating business travel activity from broader consumer travel patterns, the Navan BTI provides a differentiated perspective, offering stakeholders a clearer understanding of seasonal dynamics that are often missing in standard industry reporting.

# The Key Drivers Behind Corporate Travel

Beyond the industry metrics, this index provides a deeper level of insight by isolating external pricing factors, including fuel surcharges, currency fluctuations, and inflation. This helps reveal the volume of travel transactions and company priorities.

# Methodology

Navan's Business Travel Index is constructed using a methodology closely aligned with the Conference Board's composite index approach, a widely recognized industry standard.

# 1. Measure Index Components

We track three categories of business travel activity on our platform (air, hotel, and expense transactions) across two metric types (spend and volume) and two regions (domestic and international). This results in 12 distinct time series. Each series reflects data from a stable customer cohort, defined as companies that were active 24 months prior to the reporting month (to be refreshed monthly).

# 2. Normalize for Industry Mix

To help ensure the index is not skewed by changes in Navan's customer composition over time, each component is recalculated as a weighted average across 14 industry categories. These weights control for shifts in Navan's industry mix, helping to ensure that index growth reflects market trends and not internal customer base changes.

# 3. Calculate Monthly Changes

Monthly changes for each component are computed using symmetric percent change, which treats increases and decreases consistently.

# 4. Standardize Using Inverse Volatility

To balance the influence of each metric, we calculate a trailing 12-month standard deviation for each time series. Metrics are then scaled by the inverse of their volatility, so that more stable signals carry proportionally greater weight in the final index.

# 5. Aggregate Components

Following the Conference Board's methodology, the composite index is computed as the weighted sum of the standardized components. Each component's contribution is proportional to its inverse volatility. We intentionally do not align our index with a coincident index or apply trend smoothing. Business travel exhibits predictable seasonal patterns that we aim to preserve rather than correct.

# 6. Index Construction

January 2023 serves as the base month, with the index set to 100. Subsequent months are calculated recursively using the symmetric change method. The customer cohort is re-evaluated each month based on the 24-month activity window, allowing the index to reflect Navan's evolving user base while ensuring that growth in the index reflects business travel behavior, not Navan's own growth trajectory.
