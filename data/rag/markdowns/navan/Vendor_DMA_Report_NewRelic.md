# Relic well-being

As we opened offices in Bangalore and Hyderabad, we took steps to ensure that the more than 500 Relics who joined our teams there felt valued and connected to our mission. # Emerging technology &#x26; ethics

We’re committed to leveraging emerging technologies like AI responsibly and transparently.

In FY25, we launched a compliance program for the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, the first comprehensive regulation on AI by a major regulator. Our program includes new internal policies, implementation processes, and educational resources. As part of our commitment to best practice, we’re also working toward ISO/IEC 42001 certification—the world’s first AI management system standard.

To further strengthen internal AI risk management, we developed an internal AI Policy to guide responsible use and established a review process that evaluates each AI system based on its intended use. This process helps determine the appropriate risk category and required mitigation measures. Reviews are conducted by employees with expertise in AI governance and emerging technologies.

New Relic continually improves its preparedness for potential service disruptions and technology-related risks. We maintain a business continuity plan—reviewed annually—and evaluate our resilience through regular business continuity and disaster recovery testing. These exercises help us identify vulnerabilities and develop contingency strategies to maintain operations and deliver consistent service. Customers can monitor the status of our platform in real time through our status page, which provides updates on outages, interruptions, and upcoming maintenance windows by region. We notify customers of planned maintenance to help them prepare and minimize disruption.

In order to assist customers with their own assessment of the EU AI Act risk categories, New Relic has created an EU AI Act Assessment Guide, which outlines why customer use of NRAI is not considered to be a “prohibited” or “high risk” use case—provided it is used in accordance with its defined use cases and our Acceptable Use Policy. Additionally, New Relic offers AI monitoring to help customers gain visibility into how they use their own model and to meet recordkeeping obligations under the EU AI Act.

Our security- and privacy-by-design approach guided the development of New Relic AI (NRAI), with safeguards built in at every stage—including third-party penetration testing and the role-based access, security, and privacy controls our customers expect. To protect customer data, we worked closely with our legal, compliance, and security teams by limiting access to large language models, restricting API permissions, and leveraging Azure OpenAI’s commitment not to store or use customer data for model training.

New Relic remains vigilant about reported misuse of our platform—including misuse of generative AI functionality—by leveraging content filtering designed to detect and prevent the output of harmful content and terminating accounts that violate our Terms of Service or Acceptable Use Policy.

# Tech for good

We work hand-in-hand with nonprofits to create lasting relationships that deliver real, long-term impact.

The Water Project, which works to expand access to clean water and sanitation across sub-Saharan Africa, relies on New Relic to operate with the impact of a much larger engineering team. In California, Watch Duty, a nonprofit, provides real-time wildfire updates to communities across the state. # Sustainable IT asset management

Cloud optimization

Believing that sustainability and operational excellence go hand in hand—and recognizing that engineering decisions can help us reach our climate goals—we take a GreenOps approach to embedding sustainability across the software development lifecycle.

# Offsetting our emissions

In FY25, New Relic continued to invest in both Energy Attribute Certificates (EACs) to mitigate our Scope 2 emissions and high-quality carbon removal projects to address employee work from home emissions.

Our partnership with the Wakefield Biochar Carbon Project in Valdosta, Georgia, translates our ambitious climate goals into durable, community-centered impact. Wakefield upcycles Forest Stewardship Council-certified forestry waste into biochar by heating it at high temperatures without oxygen—a process called pyrolysis. This permanently removes an independently verified 0.88 tCO₂e for every ton of biochar returned to regional farmland.

With seven pickups in six global locations, we collected a total of 1,548 pounds of e-waste for recycling and repurposed an additional 1,077 pounds. For improved efficiency, we partnered with Firstbase to consolidate and automate the shipment, disposal, and management of our IT assets worldwide.

Optimizing cloud usage—our largest source of emissions—enables us to shrink our carbon footprint while cutting costs and improving energy efficiency. To reduce emissions from our cloud footprint, we migrated our largest compute workload—Managed Streaming for Apache Kafka (MSK)—to more energy-efficient, ARM-based Graviton instances in AWS.

As one of the first large-scale adopters of Graviton for MSK, we worked closely with AWS to implement the migration and will quantify its impact. As of July 2024, 64% of our workloads run on Graviton-based instances, which has driven a 37% reduction in carbon emissions for those migrated workloads and a 15–20% boost in performance efficiency. This shift has also helped us achieve an estimated cost savings of $9.6 million since May 2022.

The porous biochar enhances soil organic carbon, improves water retention, and reduces farmers’ reliance on synthetic fertilizers to deliver both climate and agronomic benefits. By diverting biomass that would otherwise decompose or be openly burned, the project also prevents additional CO₂ and particulate emissions. Certified under the Puro.earth standard, Wakefield sustains skilled green manufacturing and logistics jobs while advancing a circular economy model in rural Georgia. These efforts support compliance with California AB 1305, which requires disclosure of carbon offset purchases and their impact.

We use this data to build action plans and improve trainings and feedback loops. New Relic’s leadership acknowledges and discusses survey feedback during global all-hands meetings and strives to address it through company and team-specific action plans.

Our Modern Slavery and Human Trafficking Statement highlights our approach to mitigating risks associated with modern slavery in adherence to the UK Modern Slavery Act 2015 and the Australian Modern Slavery Act 2018. Meanwhile, our Security Policy sets our proactive approach to managing data security risks, which integrates security, privacy, and compliance throughout product development.

In November 2024, we adopted an internal Global Anti-Corruption Policy to bolster anti-bribery and compliance standards across all markets, with employee training on the new policy beginning in January 2025. We are replacing our reliance on costly legal reviews by implementing a new Anti Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) tool for vendor and customer due diligence. The system will go live in FY26, enabling more effective risk monitoring and elevating our compliance framework to industry-leading standards.

# Listening and responding to customer feedback

New Relic’s commitment to customer success drives us to integrate feedback directly into our product development and go-to-market strategies. We capture feedback from various stages and sources, including our Explorers Hub, technical support tickets, and product beta phases, all of which influence our product roadmap. Our O11y Explorers ambassador program further enhances this process by facilitating engagement and discussions among our user community.

We also engage customers through customer-facing roles, customer advisory boards, tradeshows, user conferences, and workshops. Our customer marketing teams regularly interview customers to produce internal and external case studies and videos that amplify user voices and surface actionable insights. The insights we gather through team surveys and industry research inform our annual Observability Forecast report.

# Partner engagement in India

As New Relic transitioned from a channel sales model to direct sales in India, we prioritized continuity and strengthened our relationship with our reseller partners. We worked to ensure adherence to existing contracts and maintained open lines of engagement throughout the transition. We continue to monitor legal and compliance requirements everywhere we operate in line with governance policies and regulations.
